# Synthetic First contact

SYNTHETIC — Synthetic Lanternfold Mutual. Fictional demonstration only.

## Scope

This handling procedure applies to all four synthetic products and all three editions. Policy terms still govern cover.

## Public procedure

Public procedure requires photographs, repair receipts and a contact preference. Acknowledgement arrives within two working days. This procedure applies to every product and edition.

## 2.1 Working instructions

Do not delay a safe emergency isolation while looking for paperwork. Record when the problem was first noticed, who isolated the supply and whether the source can still be inspected. A receipt records expenditure, not an admission of cover.

## Complete first-contact walkthrough

Begin by checking whether anyone is still exposed to an immediate danger. The handling conversation is not a substitute for emergency assistance. A caller who cannot safely reach a stop valve should not be instructed to enter a flooded electrical cupboard. Record the safety action already taken without asking the caller to recreate the event for a photograph. If the call is interrupted, agree whether a return call is safe before continuing with routine questions.

Next establish what the caller actually observed. A wet ceiling, a sound behind a wall and a plumber’s later diagnosis are three different pieces of information. Preserve that distinction in the incident timeline. Ask when each observation was made rather than forcing a single precise time if the caller does not know it. An approximate time should stay approximate in the record. Do not turn a tentative explanation into a confirmed cause simply because the form offers a convenient cause code.

Build the evidence index around the questions still open. A wide photograph may show the relationship between rooms; a close photograph may show a failed connector. Neither replaces the other. Keep original files where possible and record any crop or annotation as a separate working copy. If an image cannot be supplied, explain which issue remains unresolved and consider an inspection instead. Lack of a particular photograph is not automatically proof that the reported event did not happen.

Separate urgent damage limitation from permanent restoration. An emergency contractor may isolate a supply and remove standing water without having the information needed to price a final repair. Ask for those activities to appear on separate invoice lines. Record temporary materials used and why they were necessary. Do not describe a temporary action as an approved final scope. Conversely, do not ask a caller to leave preventable damage spreading solely because the ordinary estimate process has not finished.

When discussing damaged belongings, invite a room-by-room list rather than a single unsupported total. A useful entry identifies the item, its condition before the event, the observed damage and any available purchase evidence. A list can be incomplete at first; mark it as provisional instead of encouraging invented precision. A later amendment should identify what changed. If two household members supply lists, check whether they describe the same items before adding their totals together.

Explain what happens next in ordinary language. Give the next expected contact and what information will be considered at that point. Do not promise a payment date before a decision or imply that acknowledging a claim accepts cover. Where another team owns the next step, name the function rather than an individual’s private contact details. Confirm that the caller knows how to supply additional evidence without starting a duplicate claim.

Finish by reading back the unresolved questions and agreed actions. Ask whether the contact method is usable and whether the caller needs an accessible format. The caller may correct a misunderstanding without changing the original evidence. Save the acknowledgement and evidence index before closing the contact task. This creates a record of what was understood at the time, not a guarantee that the first explanation will prove correct after inspection.
